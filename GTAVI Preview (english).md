Exactly two weeks until release. Suddenly we're this close to the game coming out. A little bit of panic, almost. Am I ready? Is the world ready? Is this really happening?

Let's start with a few dates and numbers. The previous game in the series came out on September 17, 2013, over 13 years ago. The studio's previous game came out on October 26, 2018, over eight years ago, and I still remember that release day like it was yesterday. I remember picking up my copy and my excited trip back home, eagerly waiting for finally getting to play the game.

Rockstar announced it was working on GTA VI in February 2022, four and a half years ago. The first trailer came out on December 4, 2023, almost three years ago, and it stopped the internet in its tracks. I watched that trailer the other day (twice) and found myself wondering how a video could feel so nostalgic when it's a trailer for a piece of media that hasn't even been released yet. The editing, the content, the music. All of it takes me right back to the moment I watched the trailer for the first time. And the second, third and fourth times right after that.

The second trailer came out last year, and this year we got an extended trailer, over 20 minutes long, showing more gameplay. I've watched the former many times, but it hasn't had the same impact on me as the first trailer. I've been trying to avoid the latter, with mixed success. It's not that I'm uninterested. Of course I'm interested, massively so. But in recent years I've developed a bad habit of getting a little too excited about games before they come out. The amount of media I consume about a game before release has grown year by year. At first it might have been reading about it in a gaming magazine, watching trailers, listening to an interview with an individual developer. But then it's turned into listening to previews from all sorts of different outlets, collecting every scrap of information that trickles out of the studio, watching every gameplay trailer and every "100 things you missed in the trailer" video. And on top of that, the reviews that come out right before release, from multiple sources.

I probably went furthest overboard with God of War Ragnarök (2022). I'd been so excited about the previous game in the series that, while waiting for the sequel, I even read a book about Norse mythology related to the game, on top of following the game's previews. That, combined with listening to so many reviews, meant that when I finished the game, my reaction was something like, "Well, that was good, but I wasn't all that surprised." And that made me wonder whether my own behavior might have had something to do with the lack of surprises.

In 2023 I started wondering whether I should try the opposite strategy with a few of the games I was most excited about: find out as little as possible about them beforehand. I became fascinated by the idea of booting up a game on my console for the first time and having no idea what to expect. Not knowing what the main menu will look like. Not knowing what kind of mission the game will begin with, or in what location. UI is one of the things I geek most about in games, and I love the idea of starting a game without even knowing what its UI looks like. It's always a big moment for me when a trailer for a game I'm looking forward to shows its UI for the first time.

So back in 2023 I decided to try this with two games: Horizon 3 and GTA 6. I haven't been able to fully resist watching the trailers, but I've tried to stick to the cinematic trailers and avoid the gameplay trailer. Above all, I've stayed away from digging through articles and videos where eagle-eyed people analyze these trailers, what they reveal about the game and what's coming. And I'm not planning to listen or read any reviews once they are out.

What has made this cautious approach difficult is that every single time I have heard or seen something about the game, it has increased my excitement about a hundredfold. My expectations were enormous to begin with, but still considerably lower than they are right now. RDR2 is possibly my favorite game ever, or even one of my three favorite pieces of media of any kind. That means I'm automatically excited about the next game from the same studio, while also being careful not to expect exactly the same thing again or compare a game from one series too directly with a game from another, even if the studio is the same. So initially I was excited about GTA's potential, mainly for its open world, its reactivity, its scale and the amount of things to do. I wasn't quite as excited about the story, characters and atmosphere, because traditionally those have been very different in Grand Theft Auto from the things I've loved about Red Dead Redemption. More craziness, gangsters, shock value, parody and action. Whereas in RDR2 I loved the calm atmosphere, the deep characters, the moments when nothing happens, the beautiful nature, the ambient music. But the more we've heard about GTA VI, the more it seems that the developers have been inspired specifically by RDR2, rather than just GTA V. More realism and interactivity. I'm very thrilled about that. It's possible that RDR2's ambient atmosphere is something no other piece of media will ever surpass for me. But I think it's very likely that GTA VI will surpass RDR2 in many ways, including in a lot of the areas I particularly care about.

Maybe my biggest question mark, or the thing I'm most unsure about right now, is the story. The studio knows how to tell stories, but it's hard to ever take a good story for granted before experiencing it. There are no absolute measures for it. So much also depends on the person experiencing it, meaning my own situation at the time, how I interpret the story and how it resonates with me. I hope the story is good, meaningful, deep and memorable.

But besides the story, there are about twenty other things about the game that make me curious and excited. I love the experiences Rockstar creates. There was so much good stuff in GTA V and RDR2, so there are plenty of things I'm looking forward to in this game too.

I'm looking forward to Strangers missions. I'm looking forward to funny encounters with NPCs. I'm looking forward to heists, and planning and preparing for them. I'm looking forward to the radio stations. I'm looking forward to all kinds of customization: hair, clothes, makeup, nails, tuning cars, decorating an apartment, and generally seeing just how much you can make Jason and Lucia your own. Obviously I can't wait to play with the photo mode. I'm looking forward to seeing what major supporting characters the game has, and above all, what you can do with them. I'm looking forward to the game's systems. I'm looking forward to seeing how those systems interact with each other. I'm looking forward to seeing how the game world reacts to what happens in it. I'm looking forward to finding secrets, collecting collectibles, sports, cycling, rowing, flying and all sorts of other side activities. I'm looking forward to seeing what statistics the game tracks about my playing and how it visualizes them for me. That's another niche area I'm always drawn to. It's interesting to see just how much of each thing you've done.

I'm also looking forward to seeing how the last word of the series' three-word name has been realized in this game: Auto. How do cars work in this game? How many cars will I own? How well have they managed to make me feel attached to my car, to feel that it's mine? How much can you customize them? How different do the various vehicles feel to drive? How good a driving game is this, on top of everything else?

I'm also really looking forward to seeing how Rockstar has handled the first female protagonist in its mainline games. I'm looking forward to seeing what role social media plays in the game. I expect a big one. A very big one. GTA games have always been social commentary and parodies of American culture, and what has had a bigger impact on that culture since the previous GTA came out than social media? Snapmatic was already in GTA V. Now I'm expecting some kind of "in-game TikTok", and I'm curious about how you might be able to make your own content for it, and whether you can see content made by your "in-game friends" (the NPCs), ideally content that dynamically reflects what's happening in the game. Have they built good video editing tools into it?

One more thing you have to mention when listing what you're looking forward to in a Rockstar game is the details. Those little animations and small reactions happening on the sidelines of whatever is going on. All the graphic design made for the game, like diner menus, movie theater posters, roadside signs, car interiors, traffic signs and so on.

And then there's the graphics? What does the game look like? What does the world feel like visually? What's the rendering resolution? What does the lighting look like?

Based on the trailers, everything is on an absolutely incredible level, and what else could you expect with this budget, these resources and this development time? It looks like we might be getting one of those rare leaps, a big step forward for games. We've had those before, like PS1 to PS2, PS2 to PS3, or for example GTA 2 to GTA 3.

I wonder whether this level of graphical realism is purely a good thing, or whether it'll make the violence you commit in the game harder to stomach or enjoy. Do realistic graphics encourage you to play more realistically too? RDR2 was visually incredible as well, but it had a style of its own. The image has a slightly oil-painting-like quality, and the visual style is very cinematic. But this one looks very realistic.

But with this level of graphical quality, I'm so much looking forward to just roaming around the world, experiencing the bustle of the city and its people, but then also the peace and quiet of the countryside, the atmosphere, landscapes and animals.

It's pretty crazy knowing I'll actually get to play this in two weeks. Where will I even start? I guess play through the intro and then start organically switching between main missions, side missions and general exploration. I don't want to listen to any reviews around launch, but I'm also really interested in listening to them afterward. It'll be interesting to see how much of the game reviewers manage to experience in the time they're given to review it. My guess is that the reviews will be very good, but the reception will be even more positive among players, and then later among the critics who write extensive critiques of the game one to three years from now. There will definitely be those 7/10 reviews too, saying it's the same old committing crimes, just with better graphics.

The game's world sounds absolutely enormous. Figures being thrown around include three times the size of RDR2, and almost bigger than RDR2, GTA V and GTA IV combined. Even leaving GTA IV out of that equation, GTA V plus RDR2 sounds pretty massive. Especially if they've also increased the density of the world, how much it contains, and the number of enterable interiors. It makes sense that the world is bigger than RDR2. A car is faster than a horse. But still, RDR2 had a huge game world too, with an incredible variety of landscapes. I'm incredibly excited to take my time, at my own pace, discovering new places, new neighborhoods, new buildings and new landscapes in this game.

Beyond my own experience with the game, I'm also looking forward to seeing what kind of impact it has on the whole media landscape and entertainment industry. Every big AAA open-world game over the next ten years will be compared to this one. And in most cases that comparison will be extremely unfair. I'm also curious to see how discussion around GTA VI spreads into mainstream entertainment conversations and mainstream media, and what angles they choose to focus on. In the past, the focus has been on violence and the game's (sometimes imaginary) effects on its players. Will they focus more on sales figures this time? Or on the content of the game?

The "Before GTA VI" memes have been gathering momentum year after year while we've been waiting for the game, and suddenly, in the space of a single day, the meme will become outdated and a new era will begin. NBA player Steven Adams said in an interview in early autumn that he's excepting the standard of play in the league to drop in November because all the players will be playing GTA VI. That might have been a joke or a far-fetched idea, but either way, very few games inspire comments like that.

I'm particularly excited that a story-driven single-player game is getting this much media attention in gaming. The last decade has more or less been the era of multiplayer games, but still, no multiplayer release has attracted this kind of media attention. What this will do to the industry, and what kinds of games mid-sized and large studios will make in GTA VI's wake, remains to be seen. But whatever happens, its impact will be huge in many areas.
YouTube is full of content creators whose content revolves heavily around Rockstar games and endlessly analyzing them. GTA V and RDR2 have been picked apart pretty thoroughly by now, so these creators must be salivating at the prospect of a new Rockstar game, and how much material it'll give them to make content for the next ten years.

I plan to consume plenty of that content myself, once I've had time to play the game and experience its story for myself.

This was my preview of Grand Theft Auto 6, one of the biggest media and entertainment releases of our time. TL;DR would be that I'm pretty excited.
